# Content and Editorial Standards

**Status:** Active guidance. Revalidate by 2027-01-28.  
**Research protocol:** Follow [research/PROTOCOL.md](../../research/PROTOCOL.md). Sources accessed 2026-10-28. Search performance, audience outcomes, translation quality, and regulated-claim compliance remain **UNVERIFIED** until measured or reviewed.

## Principles
- Every artifact has a goal, audience, channel, locale, voice, intended action/learning outcome, and definition of done.
- Accuracy precedes persuasion. Verify material/current facts with primary sources, cite claims nearby, and distinguish fact, inference, opinion, and uncertainty.
- Use clear structure, specific language, descriptive headings, inclusive wording, and audience-appropriate reading level.
- Preserve brand voice without sacrificing truthfulness or cultural sensitivity. Never fabricate quotes, testimonials, credentials, statistics, citations, or results.
- Optimize for people first. SEO metadata should accurately summarize a page, not promise rankings or stuff keywords.
- Localization adapts meaning, idiom, register, units, dates, script, cultural context, and CTA; it is not just literal translation.
- Track provenance and rights for quotations, images, music, data, and third-party material. Obtain human approval before publication/sending when required.

## Process
1. **Brief:** define goal, audience, channel, locale(s), tone, key message, CTA, length/time budget, source needs, constraints, owner, and approval gate.
2. **Research:** list material claims and evidence; prefer primary sources, cross-check consequential claims, and record dates/conflicts.
3. **Outline:** sequence audience questions and key points; specify headline, section purpose, evidence needed, examples, CTA, and approximate length.
4. **Draft:** write for the channel; keep missing facts visible as placeholders and do not conceal uncertainty.
5. **Edit pass:** separately review structure/logic, facts/citations, voice, clarity, grammar, accessibility, rights, and platform constraints.
6. **SEO metadata:** propose unique title, description, slug, H1, canonical URL only if known, social metadata, and structured data only when justified.
7. **Localize:** adapt from approved source; record BCP 47 locale, glossary, regional variants, reviewer, and unresolved ambiguity. Do not silently change claims.
8. **QA/approval:** verify names, dates, quotes, numbers, links, image text, captions/subtitles, language tags, CTA destinations, and actual approval status.

## Quality checklist
- [ ] Brief includes goal, audience, channel, locale, voice, CTA, constraints, and approver.
- [ ] Outline has clear progression; each section serves the goal.
- [ ] Material claims have direct evidence or are marked opinion/uncertainty.
- [ ] Quotes are exact, attributed, rights-checked, and within applicable limits.
- [ ] Draft has clear lead, useful headings, concise paragraphs, and a relevant next step.
- [ ] Edit pass covers structure, facts, voice, grammar, inclusion, accessibility, and rights.
- [ ] SEO fields are unique, accurate, and aligned to page content; no ranking promises.
- [ ] Locales preserve meaning while adapting culture, units, dates, and formality.
- [ ] Alt text, captions, or transcripts are provided where relevant.
- [ ] Source log, unresolved claims, approvals, version, and publication status are recorded.

## Common pitfalls
Drafting before audience/channel is clear; relying on search snippets for material facts; treating copied sources as independent corroboration; keyword stuffing/clickbait/duplicate metadata; promising exact snippet display or rankings; literal idiom translation; breaking variables/links/markup; calling a draft fact-checked or approved without review; publishing generated text without factual, rights, privacy, and editorial checks.
